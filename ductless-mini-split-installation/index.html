How We Handle Ductless Mini Split Installation

Many disappointing mini splits were doomed on paper, not on the wall. The classic error is oversizing: a head too big for its room cools in a hurry, shuts off before it pulls out the humidity, and never settles into a steady rhythm. Wichita Falls HVAC sizes every zone to its real load instead. On multi-zone systems we also check that the outdoor unit's capacity is balanced across the heads it feeds and that every line set stays within the manufacturer's length and lift limits. Then comes placement. We walk the Wichita Falls home with you, weigh wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor-console options, and put each head where it moves air well and still looks right, with outdoor line sets covered in finished line-hide rather than left bare.

Our installation process is held to the same standard as our biggest central systems. Nitrogen goes into the line set first to prove it holds pressure, then a vacuum pump pulls it down to a level verified on a micron gauge. Flares are torqued and leak-tested one by one, since that joint is where sloppy work shows up a season later. Drainage is set up with real fall or a reliable condensate pump, and the refrigerant charge is adjusted to match the line length. Finally, every zone is commissioned in both modes and we show you how the remote actually works. Why It Matters

The Benefits of Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation

Most failed mini splits we see in Wichita Falls trace back to installs that treated the job as simple. The flares are where it usually goes wrong: a joint that was poorly formed or never torqued to spec leaks slowly, and the homeowner finds out a season later when the head stops cooling. Moisture is the next issue, because an install that skips the nitrogen pressure test and a micron-verified vacuum leaves water vapor inside a system whose inverter compressor is sensitive to it. Overlong or overly tall line set runs steal capacity quietly, and a drain line without enough fall will stain a wall as soon as humidity climbs.

Sizing discipline matters even more here than with central systems, because the smallest available heads are already generous for many bedrooms; the wrong pick short-cycles, leaves humidity behind, and never settles. Our experienced technicians size each zone honestly, torque and leak-test every flare, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, set charge adjustments to the line length, and commission each head in both modes. Refrigerant work is federally regulated, and the electrical side needs a properly sized circuit and disconnect. The result is what a mini split should be: silent, efficient, and uneventful for fifteen years, instead of a mysterious underperformer from a rushed weekend install.

Homeowner Tips

Ductless Mini Split Installation Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow, leaves, and grass clippings in every season
  • Schedule service once a year so charge, flares, and drainage get verified before small issues grow
  • Pull and rinse the indoor filter screens monthly, because they fill up much faster than you would expect
  • Have the indoor blower wheel and coil professionally cleaned before they grow anything
  • On humid summer days, confirm the drain line outside is dripping as it should
  • Winter defrost pauses are expected; heavy ice buildup on the outdoor unit is your cue to call
  • Resist turning the system off and on; an inverter is most efficient holding one steady temperature

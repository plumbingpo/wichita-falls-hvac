What Goes Into Our HVAC Maintenance

Maintenance is only as good as its measurements, which is why Wichita Falls HVAC does not do quick look-and-leave visits. In spring, we measure each capacitor in microfarads against its rating, compare compressor and condenser fan amp draws to the data plate, verify refrigerant charge using superheat and subcooling, wash the outdoor coil, flush the condensate line, test the float switch, and measure the temperature split. In fall, we check igniter resistance, read flame signal in microamps, examine the heat exchanger, clean the burners, leak-test the gas connections with an instrument, confirm draft, measure temperature rise, and test every limit and safety switch. Both visits include the filter, thermostat calibration, and tightening electrical connections.

You see the numbers, not just a signature: readings recorded and compared against the last visit, so a drifting number gets flagged while it is still a trend and not a failure. That record also keeps your efficiency honest, because a low charge, a dirty coil, or a mis-set blower quietly taxes every utility bill between breakdowns. Most manufacturers also expect routine professional maintenance under their warranty terms, and documented visits protect you there too. Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Maintenance?

There is a short list of things homeowners can handle: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and checking the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded give no visible warning. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks just like a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws creeping toward the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix the season before they fail.

On fuel-burning equipment, maintenance is also about safety. A failing burner, flame proving circuit, vent, or draft problem can put combustion products where they do not belong, which is why those items get instrumented verification every year. Wichita Falls HVAC makes those safety checks the core of each fall visit, never an upsell. The records add up, too: because each reading is compared against your history, the trend lines show which components are nearing the end and which have plenty of life, so you replace the drifting part on your own schedule at a planned price, not an emergency one.

Homeowner Tips

Maintenance Tips for HVAC Maintenance

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly if any of your equipment burns fuel
  • Swap the filter every one to three months, the most valuable habit a homeowner can build
  • Resist closing vents in spare rooms; the system is designed with all registers open
  • Trim plants back from the outdoor unit and give the coil a gentle rinse every spring
  • Pay attention to new noises or longer cycles and mention them at your next visit
  • Flush the condensate line at the start of cooling season before algae blooms in it
  • Schedule maintenance in early spring and early fall, ahead of the busy season and the extreme weather
